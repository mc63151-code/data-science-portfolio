Chicago Traffic Crash Severity Analysis (2024–2025)

Research question

What environmental, temporal, and demographic factors are associated with the severity of traffic crashes in Chicago during 2024–2025?

What I did

Analyzed 221,175 crash records from the Chicago Data Portal, examining how weather, lighting, road surface, speed limit, time of day, road-user type, and age relate to fatal or serious-injury outcomes.

Key findings

  • Severe crashes (fatal or serious injury) made up about 1.50% of all crashes.
  • Adverse conditions raised risk: rain (1.97% severe rate), dark-but-lighted roads (2.37%), and 36–40 mph zones (3.72%).
  • Vulnerable road users were most at risk: pedestrians (14.34%), motorcyclists (13.39%), and cyclists (7.53%).

Visualizing Severe Traffic Crash Patterns in Chicago (2024–2025)

Research question

Where and under which temporal, environmental, road-user, and demographic conditions were severe traffic crashes and injuries disproportionately represented in Chicago during 2024–2025?

What I did

Assignment 02 pair project with Tan Min Fai (MC637132). Analyzed 220,709 crash records with known severity (3,312 severe crashes, 1.50% overall rate) and visualized severe-crash rates across month, weekday–hour, lighting–weather, road-user, demographic, and spatial dimensions.

Key findings

  • Peak monthly severe-crash rate: 2025-08; peak weekday–hour: Thursday 03:00.
  • Pedestrians had the highest severe-involvement rate among road users (13.61%); bicycles 7.37%.
  • People aged 75+ had the highest severe-injury rate among age groups.
